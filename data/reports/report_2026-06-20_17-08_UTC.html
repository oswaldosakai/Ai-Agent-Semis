The Trump administration has formally warned ASML that it suspects an EUV lithography system — the most advanced chipmaking tool in existence, with a unit price exceeding $150 million — may have been transferred to a Chinese end-user in violation of U.S. export controls. ASML has publicly disputed this, calling such a transfer "impossible" given the machine's complexity and the monitoring protocols in place. Regardless of which party is correct, the dispute itself is materially damaging: it signals that Washington is actively scrutinizing ASML's China compliance posture and could use this episode as justification to tighten licensing conditions or accelerate restrictions on DUV tools, which ASML has continued selling to China. China represented roughly 29% of ASML's 2025 net system sales, making any incremental restriction a direct top-line risk. The uncertainty also clouds ASML's order backlog visibility, as Chinese customers may accelerate or front-load purchases ahead of potential new controls, distorting near-term bookings while creating a cliff risk further out. For investors, the key variables to monitor over the next one to two weeks are: any formal U.S. government action — such as a denial of export privileges or a new Entity List addition affecting ASML's Chinese customers — which would be unambiguously negative for ASML's revenue guidance; a Dutch government response, since the Netherlands controls ASML's export licenses and has historically acted in coordination with Washington; and any ASML management statement clarifying the scope of the investigation. Secondary exposure falls on TSMC and Samsung, whose Chinese fab expansions depend on continued DUV access. Confirmation of a genuine EUV diversion would likely trigger a sharp re-rating of ASML's China revenue multiple.

AMD's partial pivot to Samsung is the most actionable signal — if Samsung yields on MI-series or EPYC successors prove competitive, AMD gains supply optionality while TSMC loses a high-margin customer anchor. QCOM is relatively less exposed given its existing dual-sourcing history with Samsung.
