SK Hynix's $7.9 billion EUV order with ASML, disclosed via regulatory filing on June 10, is the largest single EUV procurement ever publicly announced and covers approximately 30 scanners destined for HBM and advanced DRAM production across two South Korean fabs through December 2027. At roughly 25% of ASML's 2025 revenue run rate, the order materially de-risks ASML's near-term backlog and reinforces management's 60 billion euro revenue target for 2030. Critically, this is not speculative demand — SK Hynix has already accelerated its Yongin Phase 1 fab timeline, pulling forward the first cleanroom opening to February 2027, and has committed 31 trillion won to that facility alone. The order confirms that EUV adoption in memory has crossed from experimental to indispensable, with sub-10nm DRAM nodes requiring EUV layers that older DUV toolsets simply cannot support at yield. ASML is the most direct beneficiary, with revenue visibility extending well into 2027 now substantially improved. Applied Materials and Lam Research carry positive read-through given that EUV scanner installations drive incremental demand for deposition, etch, and CMP tools that surround each scanner in the process flow — historically, every dollar of EUV spend pulls through two to three dollars of adjacent equipment spend. Micron is a secondary beneficiary insofar as SK Hynix's aggressive capacity build validates the structural HBM demand thesis, though Micron also faces intensified competitive pressure as a result. Investors should watch for ASML's next order intake disclosure, any commentary from Lam or AMAT at upcoming investor days, and whether Samsung files a comparable procurement notice — a Samsung response order within the next two weeks would confirm that this is an industry-wide capacity race rather than an isolated SK Hynix decision.
